# HIPAA identifiers
- Patient names.
- Geographical elements (such as street, address, city, country or zip code).
- Dates related to the health or identify of individuals (including birthdates, date of admision, date of discharge, date of death, or exact age of patient older than 89).
- Telephone numbers.
- Fax numbers.
- Email addresses.
- Social security numbers.
- Medical record numbers.
- Health insurance beneficiary numbers.
- Account numbers.
- Certificate/license numbers.
- Vehicle identifiers.
- Device attributes or serial numbers.
- Digital identifiers, such as website URLs.
- IP addresses.
- Biometric elements, including fingers, retinal, and voiceprints.
- Full face photographic images.
- Other identifying numbers or codes.
# Who must be HIPAA compliant
Any company that <span style="color:rgb(216, 203, 251)">creates, collects and transmits PHI information</span>.
- Hospitals.
- Health insurance providers.
- Healthcare clearing houses.
## Business associates
- Cloud storage providers.
- Consultant.
- Billing firms.
- IT providers.
- Practice management companies.
- Email hosting services.
- Managed service providers.
- Eletronic health record (EHR) platforms.
# HIPAA compliance
## Theory
### Privacy
Guidelines for the <span style="color:rgb(216, 203, 251)">use of PHI without patients authorization</span>.
### Security
It applies to covered entities and business associates. Requires to put into place measures for:
- Handling.
- Transmission.
- Electronic PHI.
### Breach notification
<span style="color:rgb(216, 203, 251)">Plan for if things go wrong</span>. Companies must disclose within the next 60 days after the event:
1. The breach.
2. The nature of the breach.
3. What the company is doing to mitigate future risks.
### Omnibus
Major update to HIPAA regulations that became effective in 2013.
## Practice
1. Implementing written policies, procedures, and standards of conduct.
2. Designating a <span style="color:rgb(216, 203, 251)">compliance officer and compliance comittee</span>.
3. Conducting <span style="color:rgb(216, 203, 251)">effective training and education</span>.
4. Developing <span style="color:rgb(216, 203, 251)">effective lines of communication</span>.
5. Conducting <span style="color:rgb(216, 203, 251)">internal monitoring and auditing</span>.
6. Enforcing standards through <span style="color:rgb(216, 203, 251)">well-publicized disciplinary guidelines</span>.
7. <span style="color:rgb(216, 203, 251)">Responding promptly to detected offenses</span> and undertaking corrective action.

# HIPAA penalties
## Tier 1
$100-$5k per violation; Max: $25k per year.
## Tier 2
$1k-$50k per violation; Max: $100k per year.
## Tier 3
$10k-$50k per violation; Max: $250k per year.
## Tier 4
$50k per violation; Max: $1.5-milltion per year.
